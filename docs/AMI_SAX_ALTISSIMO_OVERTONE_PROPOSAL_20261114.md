# Proposal only: model-validated saxophone altissimo and overtone instruction

No specialized saxophone fingering or overtone instruction is implemented here.
Separate approval is required before changing model capabilities or generation.

## Establish applicability before enabling a note

Begin with one explicitly identified stock alto horn, preferably the existing
Yamaha YAS-280 pilot, after checking its actual front-F/high-F# keywork against
Yamaha documentation and the specific instrument. Record model, setup, neck,
mouthpiece/reed and any repair/modification relevant to response. Do not transfer
alto choices to tenor/soprano/baritone or imply a generic chart proves a specific
horn's response. Preserve model, written octave and concert-pitch clarification.

Candidate sources include manufacturer keywork documentation and the
[WFG lower-altissimo chart](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/sax_alt_4.html),
whose many alternatives and instrument-specific comments require individual
review. Audit source attribution and exact controls per candidate, rather than
selecting an apparently common online diagram. Distinguish conventional range,
primary altissimo choice, passage-specific alternate and experimental choice.

Start with one teacher-validated written G6 candidate on the confirmed alto
model; its concert pitch is Bb5. Treat written Bb6 (concert Db6) as a later,
separately validated slice. These are candidate scope boundaries, not enabled
fingerings or promises of response. The current standard-range profiles must
not be silently widened; any future specialized capability requires its own
explicit range, proficiency and validation gates.

## Overtone fundamentals and intended partials

Every proposed overtone drill must identify a verified low written fundamental,
its complete fingering, intended partial number and written/concert target.
Fundamental is partial 1; partial 2 is the octave, 3 the twelfth, 4 two octaves.
A bounded candidate is low Bb3 with partials 2-4, subject to teacher/instrument
review. Never infer an intended partial from an octave-key label, or treat
natural harmonic frequencies as exact equal-tempered measurements. Retain the
fundamental fingering separately from any vented/alternate fingering used.

[UNSW saxophone research](https://phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/SaxTract.html) demonstrates
that high-register response involves the player's vocal tract as well as the
horn. It does not provide a universal physical recipe or prove our selected
fingering on a YAS-280. A diagram alone cannot establish successful altissimo.

## Required human and instrument validation

Before enabling a candidate, obtain review by a qualified saxophone teacher and
physical trials on the stated model/setup. Record reviewer, date, tested horn,
register response, pitch/tone stability, useful transition contexts and limits.
Keep source-supported, teacher-reviewed and physically tested statuses separate.
If validation is missing or response varies, retain the candidate as research,
with no generated instruction implying it is approved for that player.

## Exercise and product bounds

After approval and validation, enable only the reviewed note/alternate and its
reviewed transitions. Use the existing deterministic event, notation, diagrams,
account/workspace and exact-history architecture. Keep standard practice and
specialized capabilities distinct. Start slowly with rests and clear targets;
physical guidance must be conservatively attributed and teacher-approved.
Do not prescribe force, strain or detailed unverified tongue/jaw movements.

Require independent fixtures, actual staff/control/partial checks, native
rendering, desktop/390-pixel readability, read-only explanations, explicit
handoffs, exact Undo/Redo, model/setup changes and accurate refusals. No paid API
or deployment is needed. Stop at each small validated local milestone for review.
